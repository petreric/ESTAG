ESTAG: Explicit Spatial Task-Aware Guidance for Language-Conditioned Decentralized Multi-Agent Coordination

Petre Ricioppo1, Kazuki Shibata2, Yuto Kitamura2, Enrico Ferrera3, Elisa Capello1, Takamitsu Matsubara2

1DIMEAS, Politecnico di Torino, Turin, Italy  ·  2NAIST, Nara, Japan  ·  3LINKS Foundation, Turin, Italy

Abstract

Recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) have made natural language an intuitive interface for specifying high-level objectives in robotic systems. However, translating language instructions into coordinated behaviors remains challenging in decentralized multi-agent settings. Existing approaches rely on learned coordinators that map language guidance to coordination signals end-to-end, processing task-relevant environmental information implicitly through learned weights rather than explicit, inspectable computation. In this work, ESTAG (Explicit Spatial Task-Aware Guidance) is proposed: a design principle that separates language understanding, task reasoning, and guidance generation into distinct stages, each instantiable independently while preserving the same overall coordination architecture. One concrete instantiation of ESTAG is presented, in which an LLM extracts structured spatial intent from language instructions, a spatial optimization module computes task-aware intermediate subgoals, and an analytic coordinator generates continuous guidance for decentralized agents. In this instantiation, spatial optimization is realized using weighted K-means, while the analytic coordinator is instantiated using Artificial Potential Fields (APFs). The resulting guidance field biases agents toward instruction-consistent and task-relevant regions while preserving decentralized decision making. Decentralized policies are trained under the centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) paradigm, allowing agents to exploit the guidance while remaining responsive to local observations. Experiments show ESTAG coordinates more effectively than state-of-the-art language-guided baselines while maintaining adherence to language instructions. Real-world experiments further validate transfer to physical multi-robot systems, supporting explicit task-aware spatial guidance as an effective alternative to learned coordination.
